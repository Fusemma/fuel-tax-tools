# Gray fill-in boxes in Word

Written for current Word (2016 and newer). "Home > Borders" means click
the Home tab, then the Borders button.

## Once: turn on the Developer tab

File > Options > Customize Ribbon. In the list on the right, tick
**Developer** and click OK. A Developer tab now shows at the top of Word.
If that box won't tick, IT has locked it. Copying a box you already have
(further down) still works then, but its gray words stay as they are.

## Add a gray box people type into

1. Click where the box goes.
2. On the Developer tab, click **Plain Text Content Control**. It's one of
   the two "Aa" buttons; hover over them to see which is which. A box
   appears that says "Click or tap here to enter text."
3. Change that gray wording. Click **Design Mode** (it stays pressed in),
   select the gray words inside the box, and type your own, like "Buyer
   name". Click **Design Mode** again to turn it off.

Whatever someone types replaces the gray words, in black. Clear it out
and the gray words come back.

## Add a tick box

Developer > **Check Box Content Control** (the button with a ticked box).
Click the box to tick it, and again to clear it. For a different tick
mark, click the box, then Developer > **Properties** > Checked symbol >
Change.

## Add a date picker

Developer > **Date Picker Content Control** (the calendar button). People
can type a date or pick one from a calendar. **Properties** sets how the
date shows, like 10/1/2026.

## The fast way: copy a box you already have

1. Click inside the box, then click its little tab at the top left. That
   selects the whole box.
2. Press Ctrl+C, click the new spot, and press Ctrl+V.
3. Change the copy's gray words in Design Mode, as above.

## Tidy up a box

- To name its little tab, click the box, then Developer > Properties >
  Title.
- To guard it from being deleted by accident, open Properties and tick
  "Content control cannot be deleted."
- To remove the box but keep what's typed in it, right-click inside it >
  **Remove Content Control**.
- To delete it outright, click its tab and press Delete.

# Formatting basics

- **See what's really there.** Home > the ¶ button (or Ctrl+Shift+8)
  shows every space, tab and Enter. Most spacing mysteries make sense
  once you can see them. The same button hides them again.
- **Space between lines without extra Enters.** Click the line, then
  Layout > Spacing Before and After. Blank Enter lines are what usually
  shove a page onto two.
- **A line to write on.** Click the line of text, then Home > Borders
  (the square button in the Paragraph group) > Bottom Border. For a
  short blank, type underscores (Shift and the minus key).
- **Indent a block.** Layout > Indent Left. To make a box or number hang
  out on the left, right-click > Paragraph > Special > Hanging.
- **Two things side by side.** Good for a signature block. Insert >
  Table, 2 columns, then Table Design > Borders > No Border. The table's
  Layout tab > View Gridlines shows the hidden grid while you work; it
  doesn't print.
- **A box around a section.** Select it, then Home > Borders > Outside
  Borders.
- **Copy a look.** Click text that looks right, click Home > Format
  Painter (the paintbrush), then click or drag over the text to fix.
  Double-click the paintbrush to fix several spots, and press Esc when
  done.
- **Undo messy formatting.** Select it and press Ctrl+Space for the
  letters and Ctrl+Q for spacing and indents.
- **Keep it on one page.** File > Print shows the preview. Shrink Spacing
  Before and After first, then Layout > Margins > Narrow, then the font
  size.
- **Keep a master.** Save the finished form once. For each use, open it
  and File > Save As a new name before typing. Or save it as a Word
  Template (.dotx) from the Save As list; opening a template always
  gives you a fresh, untitled copy.
