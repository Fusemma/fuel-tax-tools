# Indiana: 7% sales tax on freight billed with exempt diesel

As of Nov. 8, 2026 · Sources read Oct. 12 to Nov. 8, 2026 · Open: nothing in writing from Indiana · Sources at the bottom

We charge Indiana's 7% sales tax on the freight line, not on the diesel. Indiana has told us any charge related to the sale of the fuel must be taxed if the buyer has no license or exemption.

Diesel hasn't been subject to sales tax since July 1, 2017 [1] [2]. Heating oil hasn't been exempt since March 21, 2018 [3]. DOR's bulletins say freight on an untaxed item isn't taxed. In DOR's one fuel example, delivering the fuel is taxed only to the extent the fuel is [7] [8]. DOR has kept freight, shipping or a fuel surcharge out of an item's exemption in five rulings and audit letters since 2009, none on a fuel sale [12]–[15]. A 2013 letter says an item's exemption extends to its delivery charges. A 2024 order says DOR's first review allowed the tax paid on shipping on invoices with exempt items [10] [11].

**Open**

- Who at Indiana told us, when, and whether it's in writing.
- DOR's answer in writing on freight billed with exempt diesel.
- Whether the 2010 letter still stands. The rule it read, 45 IAC 2.2-4-3, is repealed [17].
- Whether the CO2 ruling reaches diesel. The CO2 was exempt only for customers who use it in manufacturing [13]; diesel's exemption doesn't depend on the buyer or the use [2].
- From our records: when the freight line started, how many diesel accounts pay it, any heating oil sales, and any freight a carrier bills the customer for instead of us.

### 1. Diesel: exempt since July 1, 2017, whoever buys it and however it's used; heating oil no longer exempt since March 21, 2018.

> [1] [Ind. Code § 6-2.5-5-51(c)](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6.html) · 2026 edition
>
> **(c) Except for heating oil, the sale of special fuel is exempt from the state gross retail tax.** As added by P.L.218-2017, SEC.2. Amended by P.L.185-2018, SEC.1.

> [2] [Sales Tax Information Bulletin #15 (Dec. 2017)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20180131-IR-045180050NRA) · Part I, Introduction
>
> I. INTRODUCTION **Effective July 1, 2017, "special fuel," as defined under IC 6-6-2.5-22, is not subject to sales tax. This exemption applies regardless of the purchaser or the use of the fuel.**

Heating oil hasn't been exempt since March 21, 2018 [3]. Heating oil is special fuel burned in a boiler, furnace, or stove for heating or industrial processing [4].

> [3] [Departmental Notice #12 (June 2020)](https://web.archive.org/web/20201002042505/http://www.in.gov/dor/files/reference/dn12.pdf) · Part I, first paragraph
>
> However, as of March 21, 2018, **special fuel sold as heating oil is no longer exempt from Indiana sales tax.**

> [4] [Ind. Code § 6-6-2.5-12](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6.html) · 2026 edition
>
> Sec. 12. As used in this chapter, **"heating oil" means a special fuel that is burned in a boiler, furnace, or stove for heating or industrial processing purposes.**

### 2. Freight in the law: delivery charges can't be deducted from gross retail income, since Jan. 1, 2004.

> [5] [Ind. Code § 6-2.5-1-5(a)(4)](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6.html) · version effective March 15, 2026
>
> "gross retail income" means the total amount of consideration, including cash, credit, property, and services, for which tangible personal property is sold, leased, or rented, valued in money, whether received in money or otherwise, **without any deduction for:** … **(4) delivery charges;**

> [6] [Commissioner's Directive #23 (July 2013)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20130828-IR-045130394NRA) · Introduction; Statutory Changes
>
> **Effective Jan. 1, 2004, Indiana enacted legislation** to bring Indiana's sales and use tax statutes into conformity with the Streamlined Sales and Use Tax Agreement. … P.L.257-2003 amended IC 6-2.5-1-5 concerning the definition of "gross retail income." **That amendment included delivery and installation in the definition of gross retail income**, the amount in a transaction subject to Indiana sales tax.

### 3. DOR's bulletins: freight on an untaxed item isn't taxed; in DOR's fuel example, delivery is taxed only to the extent the fuel is.

> [7] [Sales Tax Information Bulletin #92 (June 2025)](https://www.in.gov/dor/files/sib92.pdf) · "Delivery Charges," page 3
>
> An additional exclusion from the application of sales tax exists for tangible personal property that is sold pursuant to an available exemption. **If the item being sold is not subject to sales tax, the delivery charges related to that item also are not subject to sales tax.**

> [8] [Sales Tax Information Bulletin #42 (Apr. 2023)](https://www.in.gov/dor/files/reference/sib42.pdf) · Example #3, page 3
>
> Example #3: An equipment rental company offers to refuel equipment onsite during the lease term of a dry-lease. **The charges for the sale of the fuel and charges to deliver the fuel to the worksite are subject to sales tax to the extent the fuel is subject to sales tax**, such as propane.

DOR said the same for exempt items in April 2004 [9].

> [9] [Commissioner's Directive #23 (Apr. 2004)](https://web.archive.org/web/20080619144929/http://www.in.gov/dor/files/cd23.pdf) · Part B, page 2
>
> **B. If the tangible personal property that is sold is not subject to sales tax because of an available exemption, then the delivery charges will not be subject to sales tax.**

### 4. DOR's letters the other way: a 2013 letter and a 2024 order put the delivery charges under the item's exemption.

The 2013 letter still denied the protest, because the casino didn't show what the exempt use was [10].

> [10] [Letter of Findings 04-20120287 (posted May 29, 2013)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20130529-IR-045130194NRA) · Part V, Use Tax – Delivery Charges
>
> In general, IC § 6-2.5-1-5(a)(4) treats "delivery charges" as gross retail income. However, **if the tangible personal property is exempt, the relevant exemption extends to the entire purchase price**—the "gross retail income"—associated with the item, **including delivery charges.**

> [11] [Final Order Denying Refund 04-20231820 (posted Sept. 4, 2024)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20240904-IR-045240298NRA) · Part B, The Department's Initial Review and Partial Refund
>
> During the initial review, the Department granted a $13,247.17 refund. … **The gross retail sales tax paid on shipping charges on invoices that included exempt items will be allowed** in accordance with [Sales Tax Information Bulletin 92.]

### 5. DOR's rulings and audit letters: freight, shipping or a fuel surcharge kept out of an item's exemption five times since 2009, none on a fuel sale.

The five are a 2009 ruling, a 2010 audit letter, a 2016 ruling and two 2023 audit letters [12]–[15]. Only the 2010 letter says why the exemption doesn't reach the freight. The 2016 ruling is for a seller that delivers liquid CO2 into tanks at its customers' premises.

> [12] [Letter of Findings 09-0457 (posted Feb. 24, 2010)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20100224-IR-045100080NRA) · Part I, Discussion, protest category eleven
>
> **The reason the steel banding is exempt is that there is a specific exemption for that banding. The freight charge is a separate charge. Therefore, the freight charge is taxable**, but the price of the exempt banding will be removed from the audit calculations.

> [13] [Revenue Ruling #2016-05ST (July 27, 2016)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20160831-IR-045160367NRA) · Discussion, Part IV
>
> Taxpayer charges its customers the following fees, charges and surcharges: (a) delivery fees for delivering liquid CO2 or High Pressure Cylinder containing gas; … **Delivery fees are included explicitly in gross retail income pursuant to IC 6-2.5-1-5(a)(4) and are, therefore, subject to sales tax.**

> [14] [Letter of Findings 04-20221058 (posted May 24, 2023)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20230524-IR-045230353NRA) · Part I, Discussion, the paragraph on Item 153
>
> However, the purchase price for this item was $2,749.99, a difference of $162.73. **The $162.73 is the total for two separately stated shipping charges, and the shipping charges are not tax exempt.**

> [15] [Letter of Findings 04-20221047 (posted May 24, 2023)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20230524-IR-045230352NRA) · Part I, Discussion, the December 11, 2018 invoice
>
> Two invoices were included in Stratum 3 dated December 2, 2019 (in the amount of $4,167.00) and December 11, 2018 (**in the amount of $3,999.86 - packaging purchase price less an $8.95 fuel surcharge**)

A 2011 letter says a seller collects tax on the whole sale, freight included, unless the customer gives an exemption certificate [16]. It is about freight left untaxed, and sales treated as exempt with no exemption certificate, not an exempt item.

> [16] [Letter of Findings 04-20100716 (posted Sept. 28, 2011)](https://iar.iga.in.gov/register/20110928-IR-045110526NRA) · Part I, Discussion
>
> **Therefore, a retail merchant is required to collect sales tax on the total amount of a transaction, including freight charges, unless that customer provides an exemption certificate.** In this case the Department determined that Taxpayer had not collected on the freight charges in some transactions and on other transactions treated the customer as an exempt entity but did not have exemption certificates to verify that status.

### 6. Courts: we found no Indiana court decision on freight billed with an exempt item.

### 7. Refunds: the refund belongs to the customer; for monthly sales tax paid on time, 2023 is open until Jan. 31, 2027.

Sales tax can only be refunded to the customer, not to the retail merchant [18]. A retail merchant isn't entitled to a refund unless it refunds the tax to the person it was collected from [19]. A claim is due within three years after the later of the return's due date or the payment [20]. For a periodic tax, one filed and paid four or more times a year, the due date counts as 31 days after the end of the calendar year that contains the period [20] [21]. For monthly sales tax paid by the due date, 2023's deadline is Jan. 31, 2027, and 2022 and earlier are past it.

> [18] [DOR Audit Manual (July 2026)](https://www.in.gov/dor/files/audit-manual.pdf) · "Sales Tax Adjustments," page 26
>
> Sales Tax (and other trust taxes) erroneously collected cannot be refunded. **Sales Tax is a tax borne by the customer (buyer) and can only be refunded to the customer (buyer), not to the retail merchant.**

> [19] [Ind. Code § 6-2.5-6-14.1](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6.html) · 2026 edition
>
> Sec. 14.1. **A retail merchant is not entitled to a refund of state gross retail or use taxes unless the retail merchant refunds those taxes to the person from whom they were collected.**

> [20] [Ind. Code § 6-8.1-9-1(a)](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6.html) · 2026 edition
>
> **the person must file the claim with the department within three (3) years after the later of the following: (1) The due date of the return. (2) The date of payment. For purposes of this section, the due date for a return filed for a periodic tax is thirty-one (31) days after the end of the calendar year** which contains the taxable period for which the return is filed.

> [21] [Ind. Code § 6-8.1-1-4.5](https://iga.in.gov/ic/2026/Title_6.html) · 2026 edition
>
> **"Periodic tax" means a listed tax for which a return or report is required to be filed and the tax is required to be remitted four (4) times or more in a calendar year.**
