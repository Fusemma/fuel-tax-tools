# Indiana freight on exempt diesel: email drafts for the customer

For the customer who asked whether Indiana's auditors gave us a statute, regulation or case law for taxing freight, pump and fuel surcharge on exempt dyed diesel, and who sent us Sales Tax Information Bulletin #92. Two versions: the full reply, and a holding reply.

Fill the brackets. Delete any line you don't need. Cites only, no links.

## 1. The reply: answers the question, then agrees

```
Hi [Name],

No, the auditors didn't cite a statute, regulation or case to us. They told us in [the audit / a call] that any charge tied to the sale of the fuel is taxable unless the buyer has a license or exemption, and we billed it that way.

The auditors' position has some support. Indiana counts delivery charges in gross retail income, with no deduction for them (Ind. Code § 6-2.5-1-5(a)(4)). DOR has also kept freight out of an item's exemption in its own rulings. A 2010 letter held freight on exempt steel banding taxable as a separate charge (Letter of Findings 09-0457). A 2016 ruling held delivery fees taxable on CO2 that was exempt for the seller's manufacturing customers (Revenue Ruling #2016-05ST).

But DOR has gone your way too. Besides Bulletin #92, a 2013 letter says an item's exemption extends to its delivery charges (Letter of Findings 04-20120287). A 2024 order shows DOR refunded the tax paid on shipping on invoices with exempt items, under Bulletin #92 (Final Order Denying Refund 04-20231820).

We agree with your read. Starting with [invoices dated / loads on] [date], we'll stop charging Indiana sales tax on the [freight, pump and fuel surcharge] for your dyed diesel. We'll also credit the sales tax already charged on those lines from [Jan. 1, 2023] through [date]. That's as far back as Indiana's three-year refund window reaches (Ind. Code § 6-8.1-9-1(a)). You'll see it as [a credit memo / on your next statement] by [date].

Sources
Ind. Code § 6-2.5-1-5(a)(4), last changed 2026
Ind. Code § 6-8.1-9-1(a), last changed 2026
Ind. Dep't of Revenue, Sales Tax Information Bulletin #92, June 2025
Ind. Dep't of Revenue, Letter of Findings 09-0457, posted Feb. 24, 2010
Ind. Dep't of Revenue, Letter of Findings 04-20120287, posted May 29, 2013
Ind. Dep't of Revenue, Revenue Ruling #2016-05ST, July 27, 2016
Ind. Dep't of Revenue, Final Order Denying Refund 04-20231820, posted Sept. 4, 2024

Thanks,
[Your name]
```

## Before you send

- The first sentence holds only if the auditors cited nothing. If they did, name it there instead.
- Fuel surcharge: Bulletin #92 puts "fuel charges for delivering a product" under the seller's expenses, not under delivery charges, where its exempt-item line is (page 2). The 2016 CO2 ruling held an energy/fuel surcharge part of gross retail income (Revenue Ruling #2016-05ST). Pump: the law's list of delivery charges says "including but not limited to" and includes handling (Ind. Code § 6-2.5-1-5). Whether all three lines come off is the company's call; the brackets let you narrow it.
- Heating oil: credit only loads where the diesel itself went out tax-free. Dyed sold as heating oil is taxed, and so is its freight (Ind. Code § 6-2.5-5-51(c), "Except for heating oil").
- The credit start date is the company's call. The three-year window is where the state stops refunding us: for monthly sales tax paid on time, 2023 is open until Jan. 31, 2027, and 2022 and earlier are past it (Ind. Code § 6-8.1-9-1(a); § 6-8.1-1-4.5). Crediting further back is money we don't get back.
- We get the state refund only after we refund the customer (Ind. Code § 6-2.5-6-14.1). Credit them first, then file, both before Jan. 31, 2027. After that, 2023 is credited to them and never refunded to us.
