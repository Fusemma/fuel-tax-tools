Work guide

Where things go at work, how a doc and an email look, how to cite, and how a state's how-to-file doc is written. As of Oct. 25, 2026.

One reader: you. Part 1 says where each thing lives. Parts 2 to 4 say how anything you write looks. Part 5 is the how-to-file doc for a state's return.

1. Where things go

File a thing by what you will have in your hand the day you need it again: a state, a company, a job you were doing, or a nagging question.

What it isWhere it lives
A state's facts: portal, due day, where the login is, how letters arrive, who to call, the rate sourceThat state's sheet, already on the work drive
How to file a state's return, including its AR report with that state's quantities and settingsA Word doc at the top of the state's folder, "GA - How to file.docx"
A vendor or customer that has been a problem, and only thoseOne Companies sheet at the top of the General folder, a row per company: who, what goes wrong, what you do
A job done the same way for every state: GL tie-out, posting rates, month-endA Word doc per job at the top of the General folder
A nagging questionThe Open questions tab in Work Notes.xlsx, one dated line each
Who said what, when: a call, an email, a promiseThe log in Work Notes.xlsx, one row each

A job doc puts the shared steps first. Under them, one line per state that does it differently, at the step where it bites. No line for a state means the shared steps are the whole story.

The log never holds facts or how-tos. When an open question gets answered, the answer moves to its home and the line comes off.

Files stay files on the work drive: a PDF, a notice, an audit letter, the reply email. Returns in month folders stay exactly as they are. Anything else about a state goes in that state's folder, in one of four subfolders: Notices (a folder per notice), Rate changes, Audits (a folder per audit), Licenses. A file about no one state goes in the General folder with the same four.

A one-time file's name starts with the date, year first, then what it is, then the status.

2026-09-18 Late file penalty - responded

A file you keep updating gets a plain name and sits at the top of its folder: "GA - How to file.docx", "Companies.xlsx", "GL tie-out.docx".

The state's sheet is the index. When a file carries a lasting fact, the sheet gets one line with the folder path. An unusual rate change goes in Rate changes with the others, and the Work Tracker gets a reminder for the day it takes effect.

A doc, tab or row gets made the first time you need it, never before. Anything with no home yet goes on the Open questions tab.

2. How a doc looks

Every Word doc you write for work has the same four spots, in the same order.

  1. The line. What it is and as of when.
  2. The answer. What is true and what you do about it, in two to five sentences. If you read nothing else, this.
  3. The detail. One short paragraph per thing, its first words naming the thing.
  4. Open. What nobody has answered yet, one line each. Empty is fine.

One doc answers one question. A second question is a second doc.

The same shape, filled in (a snapshot from Oct. 6, 2026)

Dyed diesel order, as of Tue Oct. 6, 2026, 2pm.

The President's Oct. 5 order does not lift the dye rule. It tells the IRS to stop charging the federal dyed-fuel penalty for road use from Oct. 5 to Dec. 31 and tells Treasury to defer the 24.4 cents, both due Oct. 10. Nothing is posted yet, so no dyed goes out for road use, in any state, until the IRS guidance is read.

The IRS has announced nothing; its newest release is IR-2026-119 of Oct. 2 [IRS newsroom].

Texas suspended its penalty sections on Sept. 28 for every county but did not waive its 20 cents, and 162.206 still stands, so signed statements and the caps still apply [Gov. proclamation, Sept. 28, 2026].

Nebraska refunds its tax to the hauler after the fact on Form 84AG and does not waive it; "The tax itself is not suspended" [Nebraska EO 26-21, Sept. 24, 2026; Department of Revenue FAQ 3].

Open: what the IRS says on Oct. 10. Whether Treasury defers at all.

Bullets are for same-kind things that are not sentences: names, numbers, dates. If an item needs a verb, it is a sentence.

  • Missouri
  • Nebraska
  • Texas

Numbers are for steps done in order. Nothing else gets numbered.

  1. Save the order as a PDF.
  2. Put its cite in Sources.
  3. Send the email.

No nesting. A bullet never sits under a bullet, and a heading never sits under a heading. If it wants to nest, it is a paragraph or a new doc.

Headings only when a doc runs past one screen, one level, each named for what is under it (Texas, Pay), never for a kind of writing (Background, Summary, Notes).

Bold is for the one line you must not miss, a date or a do-not, once per screen at most.

The order ends Oct. 30, 2026, unless Missouri extends it.

A table only when the thing really is a grid, like states by rate. An If/Then table for a fork counts.

The font is whatever Word or Outlook gives you, Aptos or Calibri at 11 point. Black only. No color, highlight, other sizes, underline or all caps.

What happened is past tense with its date. What is true is present tense. What you do is "you".

Short beats complete. A fact you will remember gets no line. The answer never runs past five sentences. Detail past one screen is two docs, except a how-to doc, where each stage fits one screen and the whole doc can run long.

A fact from a law, an order or a state's page carries its cite on its own sentence. What you know from doing the job needs no cite. Open names what could not be confirmed.

3. How an email looks

An email has the four spots plus Sources. The line is the subject. Sources gives each cite once more, with the year the law last changed, the day an order was signed, or the day you read a page.

Before you send, write it long if you need to, then cut it with these five checks, in order.

  1. Could they get it from the subject and the first sentence? If not, move the answer up, into the subject when you can.
  2. Delete your first sentence if it's a warm-up.
  3. Delete anything they already know.
  4. Delete hedges like "just", "really", "basically" and "I think".
  5. Stop after the last fact. Don't recap.

4. Cites

A cite goes at the end of the sentence it backs, right before the period. In an email it goes in parentheses; in your own docs, brackets. Never a link, never a footnote.

...through Oct. 30, 2026 (Mo. Exec. Order 26-19).

A quote is only the words that matter, word for word, in quotation marks, with the cite after it.

Dates are written Oct. 10, 2026. Months: Jan., Feb., Mar., Apr., May, June, July, Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.

The § sign is Alt+0167 on the number pad, or copy it from this page.

5. A state's how-to-file doc

The same four spots as any doc, filled this way.

The line. The state and the return.

The answer. How often, the due day, the portal, and where the login is. These repeat the state's sheet so they are in front of you while you file; when one changes, change both. The due day carries its cite.

The detail. First, what you need: each report or file the return uses, and where it lives. Then the steps in stages, one heading per stage: Pull the numbers, Fill the return, Pay, Save the proof. Each stage fits one screen, five to nine steps. Save the proof ends with one step naming the GL tie-out doc, never a copy of its steps.

Open. What isn't settled, one line each.

What one looks like (the brackets get that state's own facts)

[State] sales tax return, how to file.

Filed [monthly], due the [20th] [cite]. Filed on [the portal's name]. The login is in [where it lives].

What you need: the AR sales tax report for [state], and the recap sheet at [folder path].

Pull the numbers

  1. In [the system], open the AR sales tax report.
  2. Set [the setting] to [this state's value].
  3. Run it for the month, then save it to [folder path].

Pay

  1. On the Payments tab, select ACH debit.
  2. Enter the amount from the recap sheet, cell [F12].
  3. Do not pick a date after the due day.
  4. Select Submit. The confirmation number shows at the top of the page.

Save the proof

  1. Save the confirmation page as a PDF in the month folder.
  2. Do the GL tie-out: open GL tie-out.docx in the General folder and follow it.

Open: [one line, or empty].

How a step is written

One action per step, in one sentence. Any explanation goes in a sentence after it.

The screen comes first, then the action: "On the Payments tab, select ACH debit."

Say what you will see after it, when that matters. The last step is the Submit click.

A fork gets a small If/Then table, not a sentence buried in a step.

IfThen
The return shows a creditLeave the payment at zero and skip Pay.
The due day is a weekend or holiday[What this state's rule says, with its cite.]

A do-not goes right at the step where it matters, in bold. A screenshot only where a button or box is hard to find.

Using it and keeping it right

Follow it every time, step by step, not from memory. For jobs not done often, "nothing should be trusted to memory" (NASA/TM-2016-219421).

Fix it the same day anything differs: a moved button, a new box, a step you had to remember.

Re-read the whole doc once a year, and right after the portal, the law or the people change.
